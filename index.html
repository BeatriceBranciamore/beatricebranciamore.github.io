Beatrice Branciamore

Social data scientist based in Oxford

I use statistical and computational methods to study how AI and algorithmic systems shape what people see, believe and decide, and what that means for democratic life. My work so far spans Bayesian mixed models for electoral prediction, causal inference on labour-market policy and discrete choice modelling. I am currently reading for an MSc in Social Data Science at the Oxford Internet Institute.

selected work

  • BSc thesis, 2026

    Predicting electoral outcomes with mixed models

    Variational inference for generalised high-dimensional mixed models applied to electoral prediction. The approach trades exact posterior inference for tractable optimisation at scale; the thesis measures what that approximation costs in accuracy and calibration.

    • Bayesian inference
    • Variational inference
    • R
    • Python

  • Independent study

    Causal inference in labour markets

    A difference-in-differences study of Florida’s path to a $15 minimum wage, with Georgia as the control. Parallel trends hold over 2015 to 2021; with state-clustered standard errors there is no relative loss of leisure and hospitality jobs, a result closer to monopsony than to competitive-market predictions.

    • Difference-in-differences
    • Python
    • statsmodels
    • FRED data

  • University of Sydney

    Field-of-study choice under external intervention

    Multinomial and nested logit models of decision-making under policy intervention, with marginal effects, elasticities and simulated counterfactual scenarios. The model stays interpretable rather than only predictive.

    • Python
